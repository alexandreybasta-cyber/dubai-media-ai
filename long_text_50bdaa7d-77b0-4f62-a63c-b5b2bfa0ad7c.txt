Here's a pitch-ready breakdown you can take into a Dubai Media conversation. I'll split it into (1) what Qoder is, (2) what QoderWork is, and (3) where Qwen plugs in specifically for video editing and DAM/MAM metadata enrichment.

1. Qoder (the IDE / coding agent)

Qoder is an agentic IDE — it writes, refactors, and tests code with full repo context. For a broadcaster like Dubai Media, the practical use cases sit on the engineering side of the newsroom and post-production stack:
Building and maintaining custom MAM/DAM connectors (Avid, Dalet, Vidispine, Cantemo, AWS MediaConvert, Aliyun ApsaraVideo).
Writing ingest pipelines — FFmpeg orchestration, IMF/IMP package handling, subtitle conformance (EBU-STL, TTML, SRT, WebVTT, ARIB for Arabic), loudness normalization (EBU R128).
Generating Premiere / DaVinci Resolve / After Effects scripting (ExtendScript, Lua, Python) for repetitive grading, captioning, branding bug insertion.
Internal newsroom tools — rundown editors on top of ENPS / iNEWS APIs, prompter integrations, OTT publishing dashboards.
LLM application code — wiring Qwen endpoints (Model Studio / DashScope) into their own apps with retries, streaming, RAG over archive transcripts.
Migration & modernization — moving legacy Perl/PHP archive tooling to modern Python/Node services.

2. QoderWork (the desktop work agent)

QoderWork is the non-coding companion — it operates the user's machine, automates workflows, calls connectors (DingTalk, Slack, Drive, browsers, MCP servers), creates documents, runs research, schedules recurring tasks. For Dubai Media's business and operations teams:
Daily/weekly competitive intelligence — automated scans of Al Jazeera, MBC, Saudi SBA, beIN, Netflix MENA, plus YouTube trend reports, delivered to a channel or inbox.
Auto-generated rights/contract summaries from PDFs and emails (FIFA, AFC, IOC, studio output deals).
Pitch decks, board reports, sponsorship one-pagers (PPTX/DOCX/XLSX directly, bilingual EN/AR).
Scheduled crons — e.g. "every morning at 7am UAE pull overnight social mentions of #DubaiOne, summarize, post to the editorial group."
Email/comms drafting, meeting prep packs, vendor due-diligence dossiers.
Browser automation for portals that have no API (Nielsen, BroadcastPro, regulator filings).
File-system grunt work — bulk renaming, conforming naming conventions across an archive drive, generating proxy-file inventories.

3. Qwen AI for video editing

This is where the demo will land hardest. Two model families do the heavy lifting: the Qwen-VL / Qwen2.5-Omni family for understanding video, and WAN 2.2 / 2.7 for generating it.

Editing & post-production use cases:
AI rough-cut assembly — Qwen-VL watches dailies, identifies usable takes (no slate, no flubs, talent on-mark), and produces an EDL/XML the editor opens in Premiere or Resolve.
Smart highlights — feed a 90-minute match, concert, or speech; Qwen-VL detects goals, applause spikes, key quotes, and exports a 60-second cutdown with timecodes.
Multilingual auto-dubbing — Qwen2.5 transcribes (including Arabic and Khaleeji dialect), translates EN↔AR↔FR/UR/HI, and a TTS layer regenerates dub tracks; lip-sync via downstream tools.
Automatic Arabic + English burn-in subtitles with speaker diarization, including RTL-correct line breaking.
Brand-safety / compliance review — Qwen-VL flags logos, faces, weapons, smoking, alcohol, religious sensitivities before air; produces a frame-accurate report for standards & practices.
Generative B-roll & motion graphics — WAN 2.2 / 2.7 produces 5–10 s cinematic shots (cityscapes of Dubai, sports stings, weather backgrounds) when stock isn't licensed or doesn't exist. Qwen-Image handles stills for promos and OOH.
Face & talent re-aging, deageing, virtual stand-ins for archive material where rights to the original performer are restricted.
Vertical/landscape repurposing — Qwen-VL identifies the salient subject per shot, auto-reframes 16:9 → 9:16 for TikTok/Reels/Shorts without losing talent.
Pre-vis & storyboard generation from a script in minutes (Qwen-Image + Qwen-Max prompting).

4. Qwen AI for metadata enrichment of the digital video library

This is the highest-ROI play for a broadcaster — most MENA archives are massively under-tagged, especially the pre-2015 SD material. Qwen unlocks it:
Shot-level scene description in Arabic and English — every shot in the archive gets a natural-language caption ("wide shot of Sheikh Zayed Road at golden hour, traffic moving east").
Object, location, and logo detection — Burj Khalifa, Burj Al Arab, Expo dome, sponsor logos, vehicles, weapons, currency.
Face recognition & talent identification — politicians, royals, athletes, presenters; cross-referenced against a controlled name authority list (with Arabic and Latin spellings reconciled).
Speech-to-text in Arabic (MSA + Khaleeji/Egyptian/Levantine dialects), English, Urdu, Hindi, Tagalog — the actual mix of UAE on-air material — with speaker diarization and timecoded subtitles.
Topic and event classification — government, sport, business, weather, culture; mapped to the broadcaster's existing taxonomy or IPTC NewsCodes / EBUCore.
Sentiment & tone tagging for promo selection ("uplifting", "tense", "celebratory").
Sensitive-content tagging — required for the UAE Media Regulatory Office and for resale/syndication.
Rights and provenance enrichment — Qwen-Max reads the original contract/PO PDF and writes structured rights metadata (territory, window, exclusivity, sub-licensing) onto the asset.
Semantic and natural-language search across the entire archive — "find me sunrise shots of the marina with no people, shot after 2020, with rights cleared for social." Qwen embeddings power the vector index.
Auto-generation of EBUCore / PBCore / IPTC Video Metadata Hub compliant XML so the enriched metadata flows back into their MAM (Dalet, Avid MediaCentral, etc.) cleanly.
Duplicate and near-duplicate detection — collapses years of redundant ingests of the same press conference or stand-up.
